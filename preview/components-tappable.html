Tap
Rules it holds. A coloured offset block is a coloured shadow (DESIGN.md bans those); pressing the card on hover is a lift and a shadow bump (README → Hover states bans both); a pulsing dot is a decorative loop (README → Animation bans those); and a labelled pill repeated across twenty cards on one slide reads as shouting, not instruction.

One mark per card. A card that already owns a state glyph — an expander's +, a title row's ✕ — uses that one and gains nothing. Only a card with no glyph of its own gets one added. Two marks on one card is the usual failure. See AGENTS.md §15.

Know the trade. This depends on a cursor, so it fits a deck that is read — on a laptop, or driven by a presenter. It does not tell a room watching a projection that cards are live; if that matters, teach it once on the first interactive slide.
