A ghost chip disappears on a loud ground.

Ghost is correct on paper, white and sand. On lime, cyan or violet a transparent chip with a faded navy outline is a low-contrast outline on a saturated field — it stops reading. Those grounds take a solid fill.

Picking a fill for a ground (measured, not guessed) — lime / cyan / violet take navy (10.4:1 / 11.8:1), purple (7.0 / 7.9) or teal (5.3 / 6.0); navy / navy‑900 / purple / teal take white (14.0:1).

One caveat. The white fill separates from lime by only 1.3:1 in luminance and from cyan by 1.2:1 — it holds by hue, not by value, so it goes grey in a greyscale print and vanishes in high-contrast mode. On a saturated light ground reach for navy, purple or teal first.
